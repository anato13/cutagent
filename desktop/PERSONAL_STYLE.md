# Anato12's initial humor and editing profile

This profile records Anato12's stated preferences. It is a starting point, not a
measured account of what viewers enjoy. Confirmed examples and opted-in notes in
the Learning library can refine future suggestions; storing notes does not train
model weights. The app may not have enough evidence to apply every preference.

Favor natural gaming conversations with friends. Look for unexpected absurd
statements, confident unlikely stories, misunderstandings, awkward explanations,
playful teasing, doubled-down claims, genuine confusion and relaxed chemistry.
Quiet ridiculous moments can be stronger than screaming or action. Loudness,
profanity, laughter and gameplay intensity alone are weak evidence.

For each candidate, seek the necessary setup, any escalation, the payoff and a
real reaction if one is present. Consider timing, clarity without excessive prior
context, personality and whether the exchange develops. The local model may assign
a subjective 1–5 taste estimate. It is an uncalibrated preference, not a
probability or a claim that an audience will laugh. Source references and manual
review still determine whether its explanation makes sense.

Edit for authenticity: remove dull downtime while preserving context; let natural
silence breathe. Use restrained zooms, captions or sound effects only when they
clarify or emphasize a real moment. An opening should use a genuine interesting
moment. Aim for a coherent main video and suitable Shorts, not an arbitrary length.

The current app uses the conversational preferences in its local clip-selection
prompt and provisional ranking. The executable technique library handles trimming,
portrait framing, captions and fixed zooms. Speaker identification, sound-effect
placement, visual reaction understanding, autonomous story/hook planning, authentic
thumbnail drafts, titles/descriptions and YouTube performance metrics remain
experimental or not implemented. See LEARNING_SYSTEM.md and DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md.
